# A thread and a screw's length come from the standard tables, and no thread is cut

A screw thread enters a project as a thread param: a designation the ISO metric table (ISO 261's first- and second-choice diameters from M1 to M39, coarse and fine pitches) or the Unified one (ASME B1.1's UNC and UNF from #0 to 1 in) holds, in place of a value. A source's thread is the designation its quote or reading prints; a proposed one comes from the project's table, the ISO metric one for a metric length unit and the Unified one for inches, so the length unit alone is the user's answer to metric or US. The param reads as its major diameter and carries what its table gives: its pitch, its tap drill (major less pitch) and its clearance hole (ISO 273 medium, ASME B18.2.8 normal, each as its standard tabulates the hole; a size they list none for has none). model.py builds each hole and shank as a plain cylinder from those sizes and declares the thread at a datum on it; cadrev holds the cylinder to the thread, from its minor diameter to its major where it carries the thread and up to the loosest clearance hole its table gives (ISO 273 coarse, ASME B18.2.8 loose) where it clears the screw, and the report and the part's drawing carry the designation. A hole or shank is declared once, and a `count` check counts those a thread is declared on, so a stated number of screws is checked without a screw being a part.

A screw is a thread param that names its length, a length param with a basis of its own: `source` where a source states it, else `proposed`, and then a standard length of its thread's table within `tolerance.length`: one of ISO 888's nominal lengths from 2 to 300 mm that it does not bracket as to be avoided, or one of ASME B18.3's standard length increments from 1/8 to 10 in. A screw's length is never `derived`: the screw is bought, so the boss and the hole that hold it are derived from its length.

This came from a mechanical engineer's two reviews of v0.1.0. The first: screws and threads should not be recreated, their sizes should not be made up when a US and a metric table exist, and one of the two should be chosen. The second, after a run on a patent built a screw 1.20 mm in diameter and 11.27 mm long: a screw's dimensions must be ones found on the market. ADR-0007 first left stock parts and their standards out, with a deferred `standard` basis.

We rejected the `standard` basis. Which thread a hole takes, and how long its screw is, is still stated or proposed, and the report must say which; only the sizes that follow from the designation are the standard's, and they need no basis of their own. We rejected cutting helical threads, which bloats the STEP, slows every boolean and shape check, and tells a machinist nothing a designation does not; and a diameter and a pitch as two params, which lets a pair no tap exists for pass as M3. We rejected a `threads: metric | unified` key in project.yaml for a project that mixes the two, which this ADR first allowed: the review asked that one be chosen, the one run that set the key restated its unit's table, and a thread a source states is still taken from either. We rejected holding only threaded holes: the clearance hole is where a made-up 3.2 or 3.5 used to be proposed with a gap no author would answer. We rejected holding a clearance hole to the one normal size: the first run on released geometry (run 45, a centrifuge) met M3 holes of Ø3.2 and Ø3.5 in the authors' files, both real clearance holes, and left them undeclared.

We rejected a length written on the thread param itself: a source often states the thread and not the length, and one param has one basis. We rejected tables of heads, nuts and washers by product standard: a screw's head is what is ordered, which the plan says in words (ADR-0021), and its thread and length are the two numbers a made-up screw gets wrong. We rejected holding a screw's length to the holes it passes through, which needs each screwed joint found among the instances and a rule for nuts, inserts and self-tapping screws that no table gives.
