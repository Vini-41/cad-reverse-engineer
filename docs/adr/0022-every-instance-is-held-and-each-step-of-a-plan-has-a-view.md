# Every instance is held, and each step of a plan has a view

Every project's instances are held, a known-first project with no plan aside. An instance is held where it touches another, or sits within the `clearance` of one: the length param of the loosest fit, which project.yaml names. Two solids touch where they lie within `tolerance.length` of each other. A group of instances that hold only each other floats as one. `cadrev check` files what floats as `[held]`, naming it and its gap: a warning, and an error under a plan. A part made beside the object, a fit coupon or a jig, is a part no instance places.

The plan is also written as `build/plan.md`, a guide of its own, in place of a section of the report (ADR-0021): what is made, what is bought, and each step that places an instance over `build/steps/<n>.png`, the isometric view of the instances it places, in black and named by part, on what the steps before it placed, in grey, taking in the part each goes onto. A step that places nothing has no view. The report links to it.

This came from a mechanical engineer's review of a mechanism rebuilt to be printed: the assembly had parts floating in it, and the assembly guide wanted illustrations. The model was VERIFIED with a plan. Its fit coupon was an instance 42 mm from the mechanism, because a made part had to be placed to be counted, and its sixteen extension springs were tubes that touched nothing, their hooks left out. The same shows in other objects: a jig placed beside a mould, fan blades off their hub, a power supply beside its device.

A plan makes floating an error, since a plan says the object is put together. Without one it is a warning: the status stands, the report names what floats, and the skill hands it over. The three examples follow released geometry and legitimately leave out what holds some parts: a fan's motor, a gearbox's bearings, a membrane between two clamps, a mould beside its stretcher. An error for every complete model would fail them, and any project of two objects. Holding only a plan's instances, as this ADR first did, left a complete model with no plan free to pass with a part in mid-air, and two past runs did: a mechanism's springs, and a fan's blades off their hub. A known-first project builds only what the sources settle, so what holds a part may wait, until it has a plan.

The clearance is a fact of the model, the largest gap it leaves between two parts that hold each other. So it sits in project.yaml beside the tolerance, where the rule reads it with or without a plan.

Touching is within `tolerance.length`. The gearbox example's sun stands 0.00024 mm from its planets: two gears built in mesh, each flank a curve fitted to its involute. That gap is how closely the model realises a contact, which is what `tolerance.length` already states of every other length, and what an interference check already allows of an overlap. A plan on a zero-backlash train would otherwise have to name a clearance it does not have.

We rejected counting a joint as holding. The springs each had a fixed joint to the part they hovered beside: a joint says how an instance moves, and is declared across any gap. For the same cause we rejected a way to say what holds a floating instance, a reason beside it as a skip has. The warning is the statement, and it stays in the report until the part is held.

We rejected a fixed limit for the clearance. Across the runs, fits leave gaps from 0.1 to 0.5 mm and the springs floated at 0.3 mm, so only the project's own fit separates the two; naming its param keeps the number in params.yaml, with its basis and its gap. We rejected a fixed limit for touching too, the 1e-6 this ADR first set so that a loose tolerance could not widen the clearance. A tolerance loose enough to hide a floating part loosens every length check that sets no `tol` of its own, each capped against what it compares, and it is one line of project.yaml to read. `[held]` takes the project's tolerance whatever a check overrides.

We rejected asking each step to place its instances onto what is already there: a subassembly starts on the bench, held by nothing yet. Only the finished assembly is held to being one object.

We rejected a setting for a more detailed guide. The views are drawn from the model and the steps, so they cost the user nothing and are always written. We rejected exploded views and arrows: nothing tells cadrev the direction a part goes in from (ADR-0021 rejects sweeping it into place), and a view that guessed would mislead.

Whether two solids lie within the limit is OCC's exact distance, computed only between the faces that a proximity test on their meshes names; a group left apart is confirmed between whole solids, so a mesh never decides that an instance floats.
