# A stroke check may claim a direction where no length is printed

A source can state how a cable's length changes between two configurations without printing a number: a passive gripper may need its tendon's elongation as the leg compresses to exceed the shortening as the fingers close, an inequality that rearranges to the whole tendon being longer with the leg compressed and the fingers open than at rest and closed. A `stroke` check therefore takes a `from` pose besides its `pose`, and a `claim` (`shortens` or `lengthens`) in place of a ledger value, passing only when the change exceeds `tolerance.length`, the model's resolution; the quote it cites is in scope with no `values`. Every other check still compares with a printed number.

We rejected a new check kind comparing a cable at two poses, which would repeat stroke's posing and what it rests on; ledgering L0 and L1 as proposed params, four numbers no source prints; and passing on any positive change, which would count a difference the model cannot resolve. A claim states only a direction: a cable driven by two drives, such as a tendon both a servo and a leg pull, stays one cable per closed joint, the second drive a path-only cable a claim checks, not a superposed closing law (ADR-0009).
