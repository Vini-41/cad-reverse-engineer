# A stroke check may claim a direction where no length is printed

A source can state how a cable's length changes between two configurations without printing a number: the LegClaw's passive perching needs the leg tendon's elongation as the shin compresses to exceed the shortening as the toes close, L1_leg − L0_leg > L0_foot − L1_foot (Cheng et al. 2026, p. 3, eq. 1), which rearranges to the whole tendon being longer with the shin compressed and the foot open than at rest and closed. A `stroke` check therefore takes a `from` pose besides its `pose`, and a `claim` (`shortens` or `lengthens`) in place of a ledger value, passing only when the change exceeds `tolerance.length`, the model's resolution; the quote it cites is in scope with no `values`. Every other check still compares with a printed number.

We rejected a new check kind comparing a cable at two poses, which would repeat stroke's posing and what it rests on; ledgering L0 and L1 as proposed params, four numbers no source prints; and passing on any positive change, which would count a difference the model cannot resolve. A claim states only a direction: a cable driven by two drives, such as a tendon both a servo and the shin pull, stays one cable per closed joint, the second drive a path-only cable a claim checks, not a superposed closing law (ADR-0009).
